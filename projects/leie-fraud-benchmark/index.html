Do sequence and attention deep learning models help detect confirmed Medicare provider fraud?

A leakage-free benchmark of seven models on public CMS Medicare data linked to confirmed fraud exclusions from the U.S. OIG exclusion list (LEIE). Explore the results below — every number comes from the result files in the public repository.

Code & results on GitHub →How it was evaluated
~27Mprovider-years (Physician 2013–2024 + Part D 2013–2023)
3,671confirmed fraud provider-years (1 in ~9,000)
7models, equal tuning budget
15 + 3provider-grouped folds + temporal repeats per dataset

Main findings

Trees still winLightGBM and XGBoost ranked confirmed fraud best. The CNN-RNN and attention CNN-RNN beat LightGBM in at most 2 of 15 folds.
Order matters for sequence modelsShuffling the order of the same columns changed CNN-RNN PR-AUC by up to 7.7× on one fold.
Attention is not an explanationAttention agreed weakly with permutation and ablation importance (median ρ 0.10–0.16) and partly tracked column position.
Realistic performance is modestLift of 15–20× over random selection, but precision stays below 1% at every useful audit budget.

How it was evaluated

Confirmed labelsPositive = provider-year 1–3 years before a fraud-related LEIE exclusion (§1128(a)(1), (a)(3), (b)(7)). Negative = never in the LEIE. Nothing in the features is derived from the label.
No leakageNo provider appears in both training and test data. All preprocessing is fitted on training rows only. No SMOTE — class weights instead.
Fair comparisonEvery model gets 6 tuned configurations per split, chosen on an inner provider-grouped validation set. Same GPU, epochs and early stopping for all neural models.
Honest statisticsNadeau–Bengio corrected tests with Holm adjustment, effect sizes and equivalence tests. Fold-to-fold noise is large, so no difference is formally significant — the conclusion rests on consistent direction, audit-budget results and calibration.

Robustness checks in the paper: label noise (hiding up to 50% of fraud cases), an independent New York State Medicaid exclusion list, learning curves and model combinations.

This page shows aggregate research results only. It is not a fraud-scoring tool and does not rate any individual provider: at this prevalence even the best model's precision is below 1%, so a high score is never evidence of fraud.
